Jack the Ripper

One of the most famous unsolved serial murder cases in history. In the autumn of 1888 a man walked the narrow streets of London’s East End, leaving behind only fear, riddles and a question that still has no answer: who was he?

Between 31 August and 9 November 1888 five women were murdered in the Whitechapel district. All of them came from the poorest layers of society and worked the streets or as prostitutes. The murders were marked by extreme brutality, and the killer, by all appearances, possessed certain medical or anatomical knowledge.

The nickname “Jack the Ripper” appeared thanks to a letter sent to the press. That letter, signed “Dear Boss”, is widely regarded as a hoax, yet the name stuck and entered popular culture for good: books, films, series and endless debates among historians.

The London police, including Scotland Yard, interviewed hundreds of people, examined thousands of pieces of evidence and detained several suspects. No charge was ever brought. The case officially remained unsolved.
